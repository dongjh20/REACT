Additional Cost Function Design

Three complementary penalties augment the original normalized-graph formation objective in JSTP. Let \(\mathbf p_i,\mathbf v_i\in\mathbb R^2\) denote the predicted position and velocity of WMR \(i\), and let \(\mathbf r_i(t),\dot{\mathbf r}_i(t)\) denote its time-varying relative reference. An overbar denotes the mean over all \(N\) WMRs. Each WMR optimizes its own trajectory using received peer predictions.

1. Metric Relative-Position Cost

The normalized graph objective is invariant to rigid transformations and uniform scaling. A translation-aligned metric penalty additionally specifies the desired formation scale and orientation, while leaving the group free to move forward:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf e_{p,i}&=(\mathbf p_i-\bar{\mathbf p})-(\mathbf r_i-\bar{\mathbf r}),\\ c_p&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\mathbf e_{p,i}\|^2, &\nabla_{\mathbf p_i}c_p&=\frac{2}{N}\mathbf e_{p,i}. \end{aligned} \]

This term promotes correctly spaced, aligned columns rather than merely a similar distance-graph shape.

2. Relative-Velocity Cost

Position-only pursuit may cause repeated acceleration and braking. Tracking the derivative of the same evolving reference coordinates the relative motion required for reconfiguration:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf e_{v,i}&=(\mathbf v_i-\bar{\mathbf v})-(\dot{\mathbf r}_i-\overline{\dot{\mathbf r}}),\\ c_v&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\mathbf e_{v,i}\|^2, &\nabla_{\mathbf v_i}c_v&=\frac{2}{N}\mathbf e_{v,i}. \end{aligned} \]

After the reference transition ends, this term encourages a common team velocity without imposing zero forward speed. With \(\mathbf a_i=\dot{\mathbf v}_i\), its temporal derivative and that of the position cost are:

\[ \begin{aligned} \dot c_p&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_i\mathbf e_{p,i}^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf v_i-\dot{\mathbf r}_i),\\ \dot c_v&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_i\mathbf e_{v,i}^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf a_i-\ddot{\mathbf r}_i). \end{aligned} \]

3. Lateral Route Anchoring

Relative penalties alone do not prevent collective lateral drift. On the straight transition sections used here, let \(y_c=y_{\mathrm{route}}+\bar q_y\) be the desired group-center ordinate, including the mean assigned-slot offset. We penalize lateral center displacement and velocity, while leaving longitudinal translation unconstrained:

\[ \begin{aligned} c_y&=(\bar y-y_c)^2, &\nabla_{\mathbf p_i}c_y&=\frac{2}{N}(\bar y-y_c)\mathbf e_y,\\ c_{\dot y}&=\bar v_y^{\,2}, &\nabla_{\mathbf v_i}c_{\dot y}&=\frac{2}{N}\bar v_y\mathbf e_y, \end{aligned} \qquad \mathbf e_y=(0,1)^{\mathsf T}. \]

These terms suppress whole-team lateral wandering without fixing the team's longitudinal position. Here \(\dot c_y=2(\bar y-y_c)\bar v_y\) and \(\dot c_{\dot y}=2\bar v_y\bar a_y\).

The additional instantaneous cost is

\[ C_{\mathrm{add}}=\lambda_p(c_p+c_y)+\lambda_v(c_v+c_{\dot y}), \qquad \lambda_p,\lambda_v\geq0. \]

It complements, rather than replaces, the original formation, obstacle-avoidance, inter-robot separation, dynamic-feasibility, and trajectory-smoothness objectives. The corresponding derivatives are propagated through the polynomial trajectory parameterization.

Abstract

Formation control of wheeled mobile robots (WMRs) has been widely studied due to its broad applications. However, existing studies primarily focus on tracking predefined formations, with limited adaptability to varying environments. To address this limitation, we propose CFOO, a Conflict-Free Online Orchestration framework for continuous formation navigation of WMRs that integrates centralized formation generation with distributed formation maintenance. The upper layer monitors the environment in real time and generates adaptive formations as needed, using the proposed TCF-R2T (Trajectory-Conflict-Free Robot-to-Target assignment) algorithm to compute optimal assignments in polynomial time for timely, conflict-free formation transitions. At the lower layer, each WMR continuously employs JSTP (Joint Spatio-Temporal trajectory Planning) to maintain the given formation by jointly optimizing spatial positions and segment durations, improving coordination over spatial-only optimization with fixed durations. By coordinating these two layers, CFOO orchestrates WMRs to adapt their formations to varying environments and thus enable continuous navigation. Moreover, we design a Time-Varying Formation Reference (TVFR) mechanism to facilitate smooth transitions between successive desired formations. Both simulation and real-world experiments validate the effectiveness and adaptability of CFOO. Experimental videos are available on our project website.
