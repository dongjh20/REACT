Additional Cost Function Design

Three complementary penalties augment the original normalized-graph formation objective in JSTP. Let \(\mathbf p_u,\dot{\mathbf p}_u\in\mathbb R^2\) denote the predicted position and velocity of WMR \(u\in\mathcal V\), and let \(\mathbf r_u(t),\dot{\mathbf r}_u(t)\) denote its time-varying relative reference. An overbar denotes the mean over all \(N\) WMRs. Each WMR optimizes its own trajectory using received peer predictions. Here \(u\) indexes WMRs, while \(i\) and \(j\) index polynomial pieces and samples, respectively.

1. Relative-Position Cost

The graph objective is invariant to rigid transformations. A translation-aligned metric penalty additionally specifies the desired formation orientation, while leaving the group free to move forward:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf e_{\mathrm{pos},u}&=(\mathbf p_u-\bar{\mathbf p})-(\mathbf r_u-\bar{\mathbf r}),\\ g_{\mathrm{pos}}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{u\in\mathcal V}\|\mathbf e_{\mathrm{pos},u}\|_2^2,\\ \frac{\partial g_{\mathrm{pos}}}{\partial\mathbf p_u}&=\frac{2}{N}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{pos},u}. \end{aligned} \]

This term promotes correctly spaced, aligned columns rather than merely a similar distance-graph shape.

2. Relative-Velocity Cost

Position-only pursuit may cause repeated acceleration and braking. Tracking the derivative of the same evolving reference coordinates the relative motion required for transition:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf e_{\mathrm{vel},u}&=(\dot{\mathbf p}_u-\overline{\dot{\mathbf p}})-(\dot{\mathbf r}_u-\overline{\dot{\mathbf r}}),\\ g_{\mathrm{vel}}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{u\in\mathcal V}\|\mathbf e_{\mathrm{vel},u}\|_2^2,\\ \frac{\partial g_{\mathrm{vel}}}{\partial\dot{\mathbf p}_u}&=\frac{2}{N}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{vel},u}. \end{aligned} \]

After the reference transition ends, this term encourages a common team velocity without imposing zero forward speed. With acceleration \(\ddot{\mathbf p}_u\), its temporal derivative and that of the position cost are:

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\mathrm d g_{\mathrm{pos}}}{\mathrm dt}&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{u\in\mathcal V}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{pos},u}^{\top}(\dot{\mathbf p}_u-\dot{\mathbf r}_u),\\ \frac{\mathrm d g_{\mathrm{vel}}}{\mathrm dt}&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{u\in\mathcal V}\mathbf e_{\mathrm{vel},u}^{\top}(\ddot{\mathbf p}_u-\ddot{\mathbf r}_u). \end{aligned} \]

3. Lateral Route Anchoring

Relative penalties alone do not prevent collective lateral drift. On the straight transition sections used here, let \(y_c=y_{\mathrm{route}}+\delta_y\) be the desired group-center ordinate, including the mean assigned-slot offset. Here \(\delta_y\) is that fixed offset, distinct from the MINCO waypoint variables \(\mathbf q\). We penalize lateral center displacement and velocity, while leaving longitudinal translation unconstrained:

\[ \begin{aligned} g_y&=(\bar y-y_c)^2, &g_{\dot y}&=\overline{\dot y}^{\,2},\\ \frac{\partial g_y}{\partial\mathbf p_u}&=\frac{2}{N}(\bar y-y_c)\mathbf e_y, &\frac{\partial g_{\dot y}}{\partial\dot{\mathbf p}_u}&=\frac{2}{N}\overline{\dot y}\,\mathbf e_y. \end{aligned} \qquad \mathbf e_y=(0,1)^{\mathsf T}. \]

These terms suppress whole-team lateral wandering without fixing the team's longitudinal position. Here \(\mathrm d g_y/\mathrm dt=2(\bar y-y_c)\overline{\dot y}\) and \(\mathrm d g_{\dot y}/\mathrm dt=2\overline{\dot y}\,\overline{\ddot y}\).

The additional instantaneous cost is

\[ \begin{aligned} g_{\mathrm{lat}}&=\alpha_p g_y+\alpha_v g_{\dot y},\\ g_{\mathrm{add}}&=\alpha_p g_{\mathrm{pos}}+\alpha_v g_{\mathrm{vel}}+g_{\mathrm{lat}}, \qquad\alpha_p,\alpha_v\geq0. \end{aligned} \]

Using the paper's trapezoidal accumulation, the additional trajectory penalty is

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal P_{\mathrm{add}} &=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{T_i}{K_i} \sum_{j=0}^{K_i}\omega_j\,g_{\mathrm{add}}(t_j,\tau_j),\\ t_j&=\frac{jT_i}{K_i},\qquad \tau_j=\sum_{r=1}^{i-1}T_r+t_j. \end{aligned} \]

Here \(\omega_0=\omega_{K_i}=1/2\) and the interior weights equal one. The ego state is evaluated at \(\mathbf p_i(t_j)\), and peer trajectories and TVFR references at the corresponding \(\tau_j\), expressed using the same time origin. The internal weights \(\alpha_p,\alpha_v\) correspond to the implemented position and velocity weights; the outer objective weight of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{add}}\) is set to one. In implementation, the same formation-preview window is applied to the original and additional formation penalties.

The corresponding derivatives are propagated through the polynomial trajectory parameterization.

Abstract

Formation control of wheeled mobile robots (WMRs) has been widely studied due to its broad applications. However, existing studies primarily focus on tracking predefined formations, with limited adaptability to varying environments. To address this limitation, we propose CFOO, a Conflict-Free Online Orchestration framework for continuous formation navigation of WMRs that integrates centralized formation generation with distributed formation maintenance. The upper layer monitors the environment in real time and generates adaptive formations as needed, using the proposed TCF-R2T (Trajectory-Conflict-Free Robot-to-Target assignment) algorithm to compute optimal assignments in polynomial time for timely, conflict-free formation transitions. At the lower layer, each WMR continuously employs JSTP (Joint Spatio-Temporal trajectory Planning) to maintain the given formation by jointly optimizing spatial positions and segment durations, improving coordination over spatial-only optimization with fixed durations. By coordinating these two layers, CFOO orchestrates WMRs to adapt their formations to varying environments and thus enable continuous navigation. Moreover, we design a Time-Varying Formation Reference (TVFR) mechanism to facilitate smooth transitions between successive desired formations. Both simulation and real-world experiments validate the effectiveness and adaptability of CFOO. Experimental videos are available on our project website.
